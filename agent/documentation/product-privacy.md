# Product documentation privacy

Product documentation, examples, fixtures, comments and release descriptions describe public
capabilities and neutral requirements. They must not describe any person's or organization's
actual development, desktop, network, authentication or deployment arrangement.

Do not include personal names, account identifiers, home or checkout paths, internal hostnames,
private ports or mappings, access or transfer policies, or tester-specific acceptance workflows.
Use public platform/version requirements, generic placeholders and independently invented
examples. A negative statement about an environment can disclose it just as an installation
instruction can. Keep actual local configuration in private operator records.

Qualification summaries may state supported operating-system/runtime versions, source hashes,
checks performed, failures and remaining limits. They must not turn a user's environment or
personal testing arrangement into a product requirement or a claimed platform certification.
Check README and guide text, changelog entries, screenshots, generated manifests, validation
notes, release bodies and archive metadata before a future publication. Use neutral wording
without hiding failures or upgrading a bounded test into broader acceptance.

Historical operational receipts retain their source bindings and checksums in private evidence
storage. A maintained-document correction does not authorize changing a published VSIX, ZIP,
release asset, tag or signed receipt. Deliver future corrections through the normal reviewed
source/release process; preserve previous public artifact digests and state the scope of the
new qualification. Do not claim that a current-document review sanitized all history.
